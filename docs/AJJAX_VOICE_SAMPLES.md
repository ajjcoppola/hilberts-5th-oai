## Sample A — MathOverflow question

Do **not** mention AI in the body. Do **not** ask anyone to verify a manuscript.

**Title:** Which Euclidean hypotheses are doing the work in the 2026 $p$-adic exclusion arguments?

**Tags:** `gt.geometric-topology` `transformation-groups` `at.algebraic-topology`

**Body (paste from here):**

I wrote a dissertation at Binghamton in 1980 on $p$-adic transfer, with L. F. McAuley. The picture I still carry is Yang’s: if $A_p$ acts on a compact $n$-manifold, then $\dim_{\mathbb{Z}}(M/A_p)=n+2$, and the extra dimension is $p$-torsion. Finite cyclic groups of $p$-power order act freely (lens spaces). The infinite descending chain is the problem.

Two writeups from this autumn — OpenAI (23 September) and Dabrowski (8 October) — do not use that jump. Both seem to make a unit in a Euclidean neighborhood, a degree-one map or a signature-one class, and then split the unit by covering degree until integrality gives out. I am not asking whether either writeup is correct. I want to know which local properties of $\mathbb{R}^n$ the splitting actually uses.

Newman’s theorem, a chart, some form of duality that produces an integral lattice or an $L$-theory tail, and arbitrarily small open subgroups of $\mathrm{Homeo}$: if I drop the chart and keep only a compact integer-cohomology $n$-manifold of infinite covering dimension, does the same splitting still make sense? I have been trying to construct such a space. I do not have one.

Notes, not the question: https://github.com/ajjcoppola/hilberts-5th-oai

## Sample B — short public note (LinkedIn / blog lead)

I spent 1979 and 1980 at Binghamton writing a thesis on $p$-adic transfer. McAuley was my advisor. The question was already old: can the $p$-adic integers move a manifold continuously and faithfully? Finite cyclic groups can. The infinite chain is another matter.

This month two arguments appeared that try to settle the manifold case by splitting a signature. I read them. I put the reading, and the old transfer machine, in a GitHub repository. I am not announcing a proof of Hilbert–Smith.

What I cannot finish is an example: an effective $p$-adic action on a compact integer-cohomology manifold that looks $n$-dimensional over the integers and has infinite covering dimension. If you work on cohomological dimension, I would like to talk.

## Sample C — paper status paragraph (replace the template block)

This draft is for reading and for possible co-authorship. It does not prove the classical Hilbert–Smith conjecture by itself. Where a statement leans on the 2026 manifold arguments of OpenAI or of Dabrowski, I mark it relative. What I can stand behind without that lean is the transfer over the rationals, the diagnosis of McAuley’s 2001 cover $U^m$, and the observation that a bounded-dimension inverse limit with free $C_p$ fibers is already in trouble.

The remaining construction is the one I have not glued: local integer Poincaré duality on blocks of infinite covering dimension. Until that glue is written, there is no existence theorem.
